Research

At Wang-BITS lab, we work on NeuroAI, the intersection of neuroscience and artificial intelligence, where the brain informs the design of intelligent systems and AI helps us understand the brain. We approach it from the computational side, focusing on living neural tissue: lab-grown neurons and brain organoids recorded on microelectrode arrays and modeled with deep learning, with the long-term goal of interfacing them with digital systems to perform tasks.

Research areas

Neural data modeling

High-density microelectrode arrays record extremely sparse, array-wide spiking activity from thousands of electrodes at once. We build deep generative and representation-learning models of this activity, learning shared vocabularies of spatiotemporal motifs that transfer across recordings, tissue types, and experimental conditions. These models can characterize tissue dynamics, compare conditions such as disease states or drug responses, and simulate population activity for closed-loop experiments.

Synthetic Biological Intelligence and Organoid Intelligence

Synthetic Biological Intelligence (SBI) and Organoid Intelligence (OI) embed living neural networks, grown in vitro, in engineered hardware and software and train them on goal-directed tasks. We are building a wet-lab workflow for culturing neurons on microelectrode arrays, documenting how computational labs can enter the field, and working toward the computational frameworks that connect wetware with software and hardware, from stimulation and recording pipelines to closed-loop training.

Neural function in health and disease

In the longer term, we aim to use models of neural activity to study how in vitro neural networks respond to electrical, optical, and mechanical stimulation, and how they are altered in neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s disease and in neurodevelopmental disorders such as ADHD and autism spectrum disorder.

A discrete generative model of neuronal spiking activity on microelectrode arrays
Md Sayed Tanveer, Mohammed A. Mostajo-Radji, Ge Wang
arXiv  ·  22 Sep 2026  ·  arxiv:2609.23907
A discrete generative model of neuronal spiking activity recorded on high-density microelectrode arrays. A residual vector-quantized autoencoder learns a shared vocabulary of spatiotemporal motifs, and a factorized masked transformer generates array-wide activity, evaluated on human brain organoids and ex vivo human hippocampal tissue.
